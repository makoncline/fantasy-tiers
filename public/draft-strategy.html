Your next-pick guide

12 teams · Full PPR · Pick 7 · 16 rounds

Best player → team fit → who will be gone → bye coverage.
Use this order when two or three players look good. A small ADJ lead is not a guarantee.

Follow this on each turn

  1. 1. What does my team need?

    Check missing starters and weak starters. You need 1 QB, 2 RB, 2 WR, 1 TE, 1 FLEX (RB/WR/TE), 1 K and 1 D/ST, plus 7 bench players.

    If you have two good RBs but no WR, favor WR in a close call. Do not add a backup QB or TE while you still need RB/WR/FLEX strength.

  2. 2. Is one option clearly better?

    Yes → Favor the better player.
    A clear value or quality advantage can outweigh an empty roster spot. Check injury risk and the QB/TE timing guide below.

    No → Compare team fit.
    Within the same position tier, treat players as roughly comparable. Favor the one who improves your lineup more.

  3. 3. Which option can wait?

    Look at the teams picking before your next turn. Count their open positions and the good players left at those positions.

    Few good options + several teams need one → Consider taking that position now.
    Example: two TEs you like remain and three teams before your next pick still need a TE.

    Several similar options remain → You can be more patient.
    Example: most teams already have a QB and several usable QBs remain. A QB run alone is not a reason to panic.

    Teams can still draft backups. ADP and the assistant’s return estimate are clues, not promises. On your first turn at pick 7, ten picks happen before your next turn at pick 18.

  4. 4. Still close? Check risk and bye coverage.

    Favor the healthier player with a useful role. For bench RBs/WRs, check whether they can cover your starters’ bye weeks. Then make the pick in ESPN.

What counts as elite or too early?

Elite means a small group with a meaningful advantage over the next group. Use the position tier: the first QB tier, or the top two TEs in the first TE tier. A famous name or a high projection alone is not enough. An elite label is permission to compare, not an instruction to draft.

These are guideposts, not round deadlines. ADP here uses Sleeper reference data; your ESPN room can draft differently.

How to use tiers

Same tier = roughly similar, not identical. A small rank gap matters less than team fit, role and risk. The last player in a strong tier can matter more than the first of many similar players at another position.

Compare like with like. RB tier 2 and WR tier 2 are separate groups, not equal values. Use overall tiers and VAL/ADJ to help compare positions. A tier boundary alone does not justify a large reach.

Byes: cover the lineup, not every pair

Two starters sharing a bye is manageable if your bench can cover them. Two starting WRs plus your only useful backup WR all off in week 10 is a real gap. When similar backups are available, choose one who plays that week. Check FLEX coverage too.

Do not downgrade a clearly better early player just to spread byes. Our plan drafts one QB and one TE; their bye-week replacements can come from waivers later. That requires planning and is not guaranteed.

Finish the roster

Once starters and FLEX are strong, use the bench mainly for RBs/WRs who can help now or earn bigger roles. Save rounds 15 and 16 for kicker and defense. Before the last picks, confirm every required spot is covered.

Before trusting advice: confirm the assistant shows the latest picks. Keep two candidates ready. If updates stop, use the manual emergency sheet.

Quick key

ADJ: contextual help for this pick. VAL: league-specific player value. Higher is better. ECR: expert rank; lower is better. ADP: expected draft timing. PTS: projected season points, not a cross-position draft order.
